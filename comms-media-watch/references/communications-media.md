# Communications and media sector reference

Companion to `SKILL.md`. Read before research.

**Maintenance note.** Entity lists, ownership, and deal status move fast in this sector,
and 2025 to 2026 has been unusually active. Treat every list as a working checklist and
verify it against current sources.

Last reviewed: **2026-08-15**. Ownership, deal status, and leadership below were checked
against live sources on that date. Calendar anchors show typical timing and need annual
confirmation. Recheck every fact that supports a material claim.

## 1. Coverage boundaries

**In scope.** Studios and streaming. Broadcast and local TV. Cable networks. Telecom and
broadband. Satellite and NGSO connectivity. Advertising, agencies and adtech. Music.
News and publishing. The regulators, courts and standards bodies governing all of it.

**In scope only when it changes sector economics.** Consumer devices, gaming, social
platforms, retail media, sports leagues as businesses, AI model labs. Include these when
they move media revenue, rights, or distribution.

**Out of scope.** General technology news. Enterprise software. Telecom equipment
outside the media distribution path.

### Subsectors

Use these consistently. Most events start in one and propagate.

1. **Studios and streaming**: content production, direct-to-consumer services
2. **Broadcast and local TV**: station groups, networks, retransmission
3. **Cable networks**: linear channel portfolios, carriage
4. **Telecom and broadband**: wireless, wireline, cable ISPs
5. **Satellite and NGSO**: GEO broadband, LEO constellations, direct-to-device
6. **Advertising**: agency holdcos, adtech, measurement
7. **Music**: labels, publishers, streaming, live
8. **News and publishing**: newspapers, digital publishers, licensing
9. **Policy and courts**: regulators, litigation, standards

## 3. KPIs and definitions

Define every metric before using it. Comparability is the usual failure.

### Streaming and video

| Metric | Definition and trap |
|---|---|
| Subscribers | Netflix and Disney both stopped reporting quarterly counts. State when a company does not publish the number. Distinguish paid, gross adds, and net adds. |
| ARPU | Revenue per user per month. Ad-tier users have far lower ARPU but higher total yield. State which cohort. |
| Churn | Monthly or annual, and they differ enormously. State the period. |
| Engagement | Nielsen's Gauge measures share of total US TV time. It is the only common currency across streamers and linear. |
| Streaming operating margin | Now the headline metric as sub growth slows. State whether it includes allocated content amortization. |
| Content spend | Cash spend and amortized expense are different numbers. Say which. |

### Broadcast, cable, telecom

| Metric | Definition and trap |
|---|---|
| Retransmission revenue | What station groups charge distributors to carry signal. The core economics of local TV. |
| Carriage fee | Per-subscriber per-month paid to a cable network. Declines as pay TV shrinks. |
| Pay TV subscribers | Falling every quarter. Distinguish traditional MVPD from virtual (YouTube TV, Hulu Live). |
| Broadband net adds | Now the main cable metric. Fixed wireless from carriers is the competitive pressure. |
| Postpaid phone net adds | The wireless headline number. Distinguish phone from total, which includes tablets and watches. |
| National ownership reach | Percent of US TV households a station group reaches. The 39% cap was repealed Aug 6 2026. |

### Advertising

| Metric | Definition and trap |
|---|---|
| Like-for-like or organic growth | Strips out acquisitions and currency. The only comparable agency growth number. |
| Headline operating profit | Agency convention excluding restructuring. Reconcile to reported. |
| Upfront volume | Advertising committed ahead of the TV season. A forward demand signal. |
| CPM | Cost per thousand impressions. Meaningless without the daypart, platform, and audience. |
| Principal media | Agency buys inventory and resells at a markup. Central to current transparency disputes. |
| Take rate | What a DSP or SSP keeps. The subject of the holdco fee audits. |

### Music and film

| Metric | Definition and trap |
|---|---|
| Streaming market share | Define the denominator: recorded music revenue, subscriptions, or streams. |
| Box office | State domestic, international, or worldwide, and opening weekend versus cumulative. |
| Deal ticking fee | Money one merger party owes the other per day of delay. Material when deals stall. |

## 5. Strong sources

**Sources of record**: FCC documents and dockets, SEC filings including 8-K, 10-Q, 10-K,
DEF 14A and DFAN14A, court dockets, CMA and European Commission decisions.

**First-party**: investor relations, earnings releases and call transcripts, carriage
dispute statements, union communications from WGA, SAG-AFTRA and IATSE.

**Independent measurement**: Nielsen Gauge, Comscore, Antenna for subscriber and churn
estimates, Circana for music, Comscore and box office trackers for film.

**Trade press**: Variety, The Hollywood Reporter, Deadline, TheWrap, Cynopsis,
TVNewsCheck, Light Reading, Fierce Network, Telecompetitor, Ad Age, Adweek, Digiday,
MediaPost, Music Business Worldwide.

**General press**: Reuters, AP, Bloomberg, WSJ, FT, CNBC, NPR.

**Discovery only**: aggregator newsletters, social posts, analyst notes summarized
secondhand, unsourced deal rumors.

## 6. Materiality triggers

### Critical or high

- Merger agreed, blocked, cleared, or abandoned above roughly $1B
- Ownership or licensing rule changed, or struck down
- Major sports rights moving between distributors
- Carriage blackout affecting a national network
- Court ruling on copyright, retransmission, or antitrust
- Bankruptcy or restructuring of a significant operator
- Spectrum sale or reallocation
- Agency account move above roughly $200M in annual billings
- Leadership change at a core entity
- Strike or major labor action

### Medium

- Quarterly results materially off expectations
- New streaming tier, price rise, or bundle
- Station group buying or selling in a top-50 market
- Adtech fee or transparency dispute
- Music licensing agreement with a new category of counterparty

### Low, usually skip

- Content slate announcements and trailers
- Individual show renewals and cancellations
- Executive hires below C-suite or division head
- Awards results
- Partnership announcements with no disclosed terms
- Analyst rating changes

## 7. Calendar anchors

Confirm exact dates each year. The windows below show typical timing.

| When | What |
|---|---|
| Mid Jan, Apr, Jul, Oct | Netflix reports, usually first of the majors. Sets the tone. |
| Late Jan, Apr, Jul, Oct | Comcast and Charter. Broadband net adds. |
| Late Jan, Apr, Jul, Oct | AT&T, Verizon, T-Mobile. Postpaid net adds. |
| Early Feb, May, Aug, Nov | Disney fiscal quarters. FQ3 lands early August. |
| Feb, May, Aug, Nov | Fox, Paramount, WBD, Warner Music. |
| Late Feb or Mar, and Jul or Aug | WPP, Publicis, Omnicom. Europeans report H1 in summer. |
| Monthly | Nielsen Gauge report on share of TV viewing. |
| Monthly, typically third or fourth Thursday | FCC open meeting. Agenda published three weeks ahead. |
| January | CES. Consumer and adtech announcements. |
| Early March | MWC Barcelona. Telecom. |
| April | NAB Show. Broadcast technology. |
| Mid May | TV upfronts, New York. Forward ad commitments. |
| June | Cannes Lions. Advertising. |
| September | Emmys, fall TV season, NFL season opens. |
| February | Super Bowl. The year's largest ad event. |
| Rolling | Sports rights windows, carriage renewal deadlines, court dates. |

The FCC open meeting agenda and the earnings calendar are the two anchors most often
missed. Check both before writing "What to watch next."

## 8. Search terms and aliases

**Entity aliases**: Paramount Skydance was Paramount Global, and before that ViacomCBS.
Warner Bros. Discovery is often WBD or Warner. Versant covers the former NBCUniversal
cable networks, and MS NOW was MSNBC until 2025. TEGNA is now inside Nexstar. Omnicom
absorbed Interpublic, so legacy IPG agency names still appear.

**Useful query patterns**: `<company> 8-K <topic>`; `site:docs.fcc.gov <docket>`;
`<company> earnings call transcript <quarter>`; `<company> v <company> complaint`;
`<network> carriage dispute <distributor>`; `<brand> media review agency of record`;
`<league> media rights <year>`.

**Time-window discipline**: always include the year. Media coverage of long-running
sagas surfaces old articles that read as current.

## 9. Plain-language glossary

Use these glosses, or better ones, on first use. Consistency matters more than elegance.

| Term | Plain gloss |
|---|---|
| Linear TV | Traditional scheduled television, cable or broadcast. |
| Cord-cutting | Households dropping cable or satellite TV. |
| MVPD | A pay TV distributor such as Comcast or DirecTV. |
| vMVPD | A streaming pay TV bundle such as YouTube TV. |
| Retransmission fee | What a local station charges a distributor to carry its signal. |
| Carriage fee | What a cable network charges per subscriber per month. |
| Blackout | A channel pulled during a carriage dispute. |
| SVOD | Subscription streaming, such as Netflix. |
| AVOD | Free ad-supported streaming. |
| FAST channel | A free streaming channel that plays on a schedule, like old cable. |
| Ownership cap | The old limit on how many US homes one TV owner could reach. Repealed Aug 2026. |
| Upfronts | The May event where networks sell next season's advertising in advance. |
| Scatter market | Advertising bought closer to air, outside the upfront. |
| CPM | The price of reaching a thousand viewers. |
| Holdco | An agency holding company owning many agency brands. |
| DSP | Software advertisers use to buy ads automatically. |
| Principal media | An agency buying ad space itself and reselling it at a markup. |
| ARPU | Average revenue per user per month. |
| Churn | The share of customers who cancel. |
| Net adds | Customers gained minus customers lost. |
| Fixed wireless | Home internet delivered over a mobile network. |
| MVNO | A company that provides service through rented network capacity. |
| Direct-to-device | Satellites connecting to ordinary phones without special hardware. |
| GEO and LEO | High orbit versus low orbit satellites. Low orbit is faster. |
| Spectrum | The airwaves licensed for wireless services. |
| Ticking fee | Money a merger party owes per day while a deal stays unfinished. |
| Public service broadcasting | Programming obligations attached to a broadcast licence, mainly UK. |
| Windowing | Releasing content in stages, cinema then streaming. |
| Residuals | Payments to talent when content is reused. |
